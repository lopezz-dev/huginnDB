# Convention #82: Dialogs have three tiers and one seam contract, decided in `ui/dialog.tsx`

Every `DialogContent` picks a `tier` — `prompt` (8px, one-line confirms), `panel` (10px, the ~18 forms, the default), or `workbench` (edge-to-edge, borrows the workspace island's own border/fill/`shadow-island`) — and `DialogHeader`/`DialogBody`/`DialogFooter` read that tier from a private context to render their own chrome. A call site says one word and cannot re-declare the rail, the padding, the radius or the close-button gap. `window.confirm` is gone too, replaced by an async `ConfirmHost` over a `prompt`-tier dialog, and the tab strip is now coupled to its panel instead of floating over a trench.



## Detail

- **The tier is a prop, not something derived from `children` at runtime.** ADR #64's `surfaceFor` pattern is for a decision only knowable from content at render time (does this notification fit on one line); a dialog's tier is knowable statically by whoever writes the call site. Inspecting `React.Children` to guess it would break the moment someone wraps the body in a fragment, silently, as a wrong anatomy rather than a build error.
- **There was a fourth, transitional `padded` variant, and it is deliberately gone now.** It reproduced the pre-tier single anatomy (`max-w-md gap-4 rounded-lg p-6 shadow-elevation-4`) byte for byte, so introducing the other three tiers was a visual-change-zero commit — no call site passed `tier` yet, so nothing on screen moved. `uiAdoption.test.ts` tracked it as a census (call sites still missing `tier=`), driven to `{}` across six domain-migration commits; once it hit zero, `padded` and its census were deleted in the same commit, and `defaultVariants.tier` flipped to `panel`. The census reached zero one commit earlier than the design doc that planned this work expected (it estimated 27 call sites; the real, measured count was 34, and every one of them was accounted for by the time the `prompt`-tier commit finished) — a live example of "recount before trusting the plan's own arithmetic."
- **`panel` and `workbench` share one rail recipe** (`border-b border-border px-5 py-3 pr-10` on the header, `border-t border-border px-5 py-3` on the footer) — this is what let the six-domain migration assign either tier to a call site without ever touching its header/footer markup twice. `prompt` has no rail at all: a one-row header (`flex-row items-center gap-3 p-4 pb-2 pr-10`, no stacked description slot) and the description moved into the body instead.
- **The seam this whole system exists to close: seven dialogs hand-copied their header rail, in five spellings, before this.** `uiContracts.test.ts`'s "the modal plane is declared once" rule guards the half of that seam a regex actually can enforce without an allowlist: no `DialogPrimitive.Overlay` and no `bg-scrim` outside `ui/dialog.tsx` (true from the day `shell/OverlayPalette.tsx` started importing `DialogOverlay` instead of hand-copying it), plus no literal `bg-black/*` anywhere in `components/`. **The rule does *not* extend to banning every padding/gap/radius/border className on `DialogContent`/`DialogHeader`/`DialogFooter`/`DialogBody`, even though the design doc that drove this work wanted exactly that.** Written and measured against the real tree, it had sixteen counterexamples that are legitimate content, not leftovers: `SaveViewDialog`'s `DialogBody` needs its own `gap-3` between two form fields (`DialogBody` never fixes a gap of its own, so this never duplicates tier chrome); `CellEditor`'s header needs `gap-2` between an icon and a title (internal flex layout, not the rail); `CellEditor`'s fullscreen mode needs `rounded-none` on `DialogContent` to *override* `workbench`'s `rounded-lg`; `WhatsNewDialog`'s banner needs `DialogBody`'s `p-0` precisely because it is the one documented place that doesn't fit the tier's rhythm. A prefix ban can't tell "duplicates the tier's own rail" apart from "content the consumer legitimately owns" without an allowlist, and `uiContracts.test.ts`'s own header rule is that a rule needing exceptions doesn't belong in it. Left undone rather than merged broken — see that file's own comment on rule J.
- **`WhatsNewDialog`'s brand banner is the one dialog that doesn't fit any tier's rhythm, and it is not modeled as a prop.** Its `px-7` banner (sticker mark, halftone wash, version pill) replaces `DialogHeader` entirely — there is no header slot for it — and becomes `DialogBody`'s first child instead, with `DialogBody` itself given `p-0` so the banner and the highlights list beneath it keep their own padding instead of the tier's `px-5`. The footer restates `px-7 py-4` over the tier's `px-5 py-3` for the same reason, keeping only the tier's `border-t border-border`. The alternative — a `banner` prop on `DialogContent` — was rejected for having exactly one consumer.
- **`DialogActions` no longer takes a `size` prop.** It used to default to `"md"`, with roughly half the app's footers passing `"sm"` explicitly and half taking the default — a divergence carried in the component rather than ever settled. `sm` is now the only size, hard-coded, and Cancel converges on `variant="ghost"` in the eight hand-rolled footers (outside `DialogActions`) that still used `"outline"` for it — `DialogActions` itself had always used `ghost`, so this closes the last of a divergence, not a new decision.
- **`window.confirm` is gone, replaced by `ConfirmHost` — a `tier="prompt"` dialog over `stores/dialogs/confirmRequest.ts`.** `lib/confirmDestructive.ts`'s two exports (`confirmDestructive`, `confirmIrreversible`) kept their names and their `ui.confirmDestructive` gate but now return `Promise<boolean>`. The store's `queue` is the one thing a blocking `window.confirm` never had to think about: a second confirm request arriving while one is already open. It queues rather than replacing `current` (the request `ConfirmHost` actually renders), so the first caller's promise is always answered before the next request is even shown — see `confirmRequest.test.ts`. `ConfirmHost` itself carries no `submitting` state: the caller does its own work *after* the promise resolves, which is what keeps `useAsyncSubmit`'s deliberate "stay busy after success" (ADR #44) intact; awaiting inside the host would race it. Mounted once per window (`App.tsx`, `pulse/PulseWindow.tsx`, `shell/DetachedTabWindow.tsx` — required there, since `TableDataTab`/`DocumentListView` both call `confirmDestructive` from a detached tab, and a window with no host would leave that promise unsettled forever).
- **An async confirm opens a gap a blocking one never could: state can change while the user is still deciding.** `window.confirm` froze the whole tab; nothing on screen could re-render while it was up. `DocumentListView.tsx`'s `deleteField` is the one call site this actually bites, because it closes over `rowValues` (a prop of the memoised `DocumentCard`), and that row can now refresh underneath the open dialog. Fixed with the same idiom `callbacksRef` two lines up already uses: a `rowValuesRef`, kept current every render, read after the `await` instead of the `rowValues` the function's own closure captured at call time.
- **The tab strip is coupled to its panel, not floating over a trench.** The active chip's radius is top-only (`8px 8px 0 0`, scoped to `.dv-tabs-container.dv-horizontal .dv-tab` so the overflow popover's own stacked rows — which reuse the same `.dv-tab` markup on purpose — keep their plain 4-corner shape), its bottom seam is painted over with a `::after` in the panel's own background colour (covering both the strip's `border-bottom` and the chip's own now-transparent `border-bottom-color`, since painting over the seam is more reliable than making a margin swallow a border owned by the parent), inactive chips lose their fill in the strip only (the popover's rows keep theirs — each is its own standalone card there), and the two `filter: drop-shadow(...)` lift layers are gone (a coupled tab does not also float above what it merges into). `ui.tabAccentStyle` (cap/rail/boxed) and the per-tab custom colour are unaffected — neither ever depended on radius, fill or lift.
- **The accent stripe (cap/rail/boxed) and a semantic-rail-plus-medallion treatment (`shell/notificationVisuals.ts`'s `NotificationCard` device) are a deliberate divergence, not an oversight.** The tab crest means "this tab is selected," is a user preference (`ui.tabAccentStyle`), and `ui/` cannot read preferences at all (ADR #64's own dependency rule) or the `NotificationKind` enum a notification-tone medallion would need — the key sets don't even line up (`destructive` for a dialog, `error` for a notification; `file`/`progress`/`success` mean nothing in a dialog). The two systems are allowed to look different on purpose, the same way convention #45 documents its own deliberate divergence.
